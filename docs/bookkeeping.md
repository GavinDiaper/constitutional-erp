# **📘 Introduction to Bookkeeping & the General Ledger**

Bookkeeping is the systematic recording of a business’s financial transactions. It forms the foundation of accounting, ensuring that every financial event is captured, classified, and organized for reporting and decision‑making.

---

## **📚 Core Concepts**

### **1. The Accounting Equation**
At the heart of bookkeeping is the fundamental equation:

\[
\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}
\]

Every transaction must keep this equation in balance.

---

### **2. Double‑Entry Bookkeeping**
Every transaction affects **at least two accounts**:

- One **debit**
- One **credit**

And:

\[
\text{Total Debits} = \text{Total Credits}
\]

This system ensures accuracy and reduces errors.

---

## **🧾 Debits and Credits**

### **Debit (Dr)**
- Increases: **Assets**, **Expenses**
- Decreases: **Liabilities**, **Equity**, **Revenue**

### **Credit (Cr)**
- Increases: **Liabilities**, **Equity**, **Revenue**
- Decreases: **Assets**, **Expenses**

### **Quick Reference Table**

| Account Type | Debit Effect | Credit Effect |
|--------------|--------------|----------------|
| Assets       | Increase     | Decrease       |
| Liabilities  | Decrease     | Increase       |
| Equity       | Decrease     | Increase       |
| Revenue      | Decrease     | Increase       |
| Expenses     | Increase     | Decrease       |

---

## **📒 Journals and Journal Entries**

Transactions are first recorded in the **general journal**.

### **Structure of a Journal Entry**
```
Date        Account Debited        Dr Amount
            Account Credited           Cr Amount
Narration (brief explanation)
```

### **Example**
```
2026-04-02   Office Supplies          500
             Cash                         500
Purchased office supplies for cash.
```

---

## **📘 Posting to the General Ledger**

The **General Ledger (GL)** is a collection of all accounts used by the business.  
Posting transfers information from the **journal** to the **ledger**.

### **Steps to Post**
1. Identify accounts involved in the journal entry.
2. Locate each account’s ledger page (T‑account or running balance format).
3. Post the **debit** to the debit side of the account.
4. Post the **credit** to the credit side of the account.
5. Reference the journal entry number for traceability.

### **Example T‑Accounts**

**Office Supplies (Asset)**  
```
Date        Description     Dr     Cr     Balance
2026-04-02  JE#1            500            500 Dr
```

**Cash (Asset)**  
```
Date        Description     Dr     Cr     Balance
2026-04-02  JE#1                   500     500 Cr
```

---

## **📊 Trial Balance**

A **Trial Balance** is prepared to ensure that total debits equal total credits after posting.

### **Purpose**
- Detect posting or arithmetic errors
- Provide a snapshot of account balances
- Prepare financial statements

### **Format**
| Account Name     | Debit | Credit |
|------------------|-------|--------|
| Cash             |       | 500    |
| Office Supplies  | 500   |        |
| **Totals**       | **500** | **500** |

If the totals match, the ledger is arithmetically sound (though not necessarily error‑free).

---

## **🧠 Common Errors Not Caught by a Trial Balance**
Even if debits = credits, these errors may still exist:

- Posting to the wrong account  
- Omitting a transaction  
- Recording the wrong amount in both Dr and Cr  
- Compensating errors (two unrelated mistakes cancel out)
